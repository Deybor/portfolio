# Amara: a quicker, more satisfying study

Research and live-reference review, 1 October 2026. This memo guides local portfolio refinement; it does not establish jewellery production status or hiring outcomes.

## Evidence and its limits

**Make the work easy to assess.** Nielsen Norman Group surveyed 204 UX professionals responsible for hiring. Its portfolio guidance favors scannable stories showing the problem, personal contribution, constraints, decisions and supporting work. It cautions against unnecessary detail and difficult navigation. This is UX hiring evidence, not direct jewellery hiring research; applying it to a jewellery reviewer is an inference. No fixed recruiter attention window or engagement uplift is claimed. [Source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/)

**Show that the story continues.** NN/G identifies large heroes, expansive gaps and strong horizontal boundaries as possible “false bottoms”: readers can mistake the visible content for the end. A glimpse of the next section and clear continuation cues improve discoverability. For Amara, a named next-chapter link and a connecting visual thread should make the next step apparent on both desktop and mobile. [Source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/illusion-of-completeness/)

**Give motion a job.** NN/G's qualitative scroll-fading study supports responsive, sparse effects whose content remains available after appearing. Repeatedly disappearing content forces readers to hunt for the correct scroll position. Its earlier research recommends animating supporting imagery rather than delaying the main text. Keep native scrolling and readable copy; let the stud reveal and section transitions carry the expressive motion. [Scroll-fading study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-fading-101/), [text-animation research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-animations/)

**Respect motion preferences.** W3C's guidance for interaction-triggered motion explains why nonessential animation should be disableable. Use the device's reduced-motion preference to show the complete story immediately with static imagery and no parallax or smooth-scroll dependency. This is an implementation decision, not a claim of complete accessibility certification. [Source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

## Motion refinement after visual review

The opening stays as approved. This pass removes written scrolling prompts and decorative arrows, restores the original charcoal-to-amber gradient, and replaces the small entrance offsets with visible scene choreography.

- [Longines Spirit Flyback, Immersive Garden](https://immersive-g.com/projects/longines-flyback/): an award-recognized product experience connecting atmosphere, heritage and technical detail. Amara borrows continuous transitions between scenes; the soft image-edge mask is our adaptation.
- [Magnetic Image Reveal, Made With GSAP](https://madewithgsap.com/effects/tutorial096): the public tutorial describes images arriving progressively and settling into a composition. Amara uses that arrival-and-settle idea for one existing stud render.
- [Mathijs Hanenkamp, maker's case study](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/mathijs-hanenkamp-photography-portfolio-case-study): documents one-time eased photography and editorial entrances. Amara uses separate image and text stages.
- [GSAP stagger documentation](https://gsap.com/resources/getting-started/Staggers/): reference for deliberate sequencing. Amara's table slides in from the right, then its actual values enter in small batches. Values are never counted through incorrect intermediate specifications.

The desktop Nest scene fades and enlarges on approach, moves aside, then reveals its explanation. Mobile and shorter views retain a visible fade/scale entrance without a constrained sticky stage. Once revealed, text and table values stay readable. The image blends into the warm field; the field blends into the cream Model section. Reduced-motion preferences show the complete static content. Native scrolling remains in use.
